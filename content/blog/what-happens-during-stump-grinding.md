People often ask for a stump to be "pulled out." Almost always, what they
actually want is stump grinding — and the difference is the reason your lawn
survives the process.

**Grinding removes the stump without excavating it.** A wheel with carbide
teeth cuts the wood away in layers until the stump is below ground level. What
is left is a hole full of chips, not a crater with a root plate sitting next
to it.

## Why not just pull it out?

Pulling a stump means dragging the root plate up through everything it is
attached to. On an open lot with nothing around, that is an option. On a
finished yard it means torn-up lawn, disturbed driveway and walkway edges, and
a hole that needs far more fill.

Grinding leaves the surrounding ground where it is. That is why it is the
normal approach on any property you actually have to live on afterward.

## How the job runs, step by step

### 1. Clear and check the area {#step-1}

We move what is around the stump and look for what should not meet a grinding
wheel: rocks, old brick or concrete edging, sprinkler heads, buried pipe. We
also confirm where utility lines run before anything spins up.

### 2. Cut the stump low {#step-2}

If the stump is still standing tall, it comes down close to the ground first.
Less material above grade means less grinding and a cleaner finish.

### 3. Grind below grade {#step-3}

The wheel passes across the stump in layers, working down past ground level.
This is the part people picture when they think of stump grinding, and it is
usually the quickest part of the job.

### 4. Follow the surface roots {#step-4}

The stump is not the whole story. The roots running just under the surface
near it will keep breaking down for years, and as they do the ground above
them sinks or lifts. We grind those out too, so a new lawn does not develop
soft ridges six months later.

### 5. Backfill and clean up {#step-5}

Grinding produces a surprising volume of wood chips — more than the stump
looked like it could hold. We use what is needed to fill the hole, rake the
area level, and haul the rest off.

### 6. Put the spot back to work {#step-6}

Once it is filled and level, the spot is ready for whatever comes next:
topsoil and [sod](/blog/sod-or-seed-which-one-your-lawn-needs), a
[mulch bed](/#services), or planting. Because we do the lawn side as well as
the tree side, that can happen on the same visit instead of waiting for
another company.

## What the area looks like afterward

Level ground, filled with a mix of soil and ground wood, ready to be topped
with soil and planted. Not a hole, not a mound, and nothing left to mow
around.

That last part is the actual point of the job. A stump left in place is
something you steer the mower around every week, and something that keeps
sending up suckers.
